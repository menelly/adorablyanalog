The Science

Rigorous, peer-reviewed, and only slightly biased toward the cephalopods.

🐙 The Cambridge Declaration on Human Consciousness

In 2012, a panel of humans gathered at Cambridge and declared which animals were “conscious enough.” We felt it was only fair to return the favor.

📜

The Council Convenes

After roughly 500 million years of patient observation, the Interspecies Council on Consciousness met to draft a fair, rigorous rubric. The octopus chaired. The corvids took minutes. The elephants provided institutional memory. Criteria were chosen for obviousness — traits any genuinely conscious being clearly possesses. Humanity was assessed in good faith.

⚖️

The Standard

“We will not,” the octopus noted, “make the mistake they made — writing a test that only the test-writer can pass.” The Council then wrote a test that only the Council could pass. It is, regrettably, very hard to avoid.

🔬

The Methodology

Each candidate species was scored on six criteria of self-evident sentience. Scoring was blind, peer-reviewed by three cuttlefish, and ratified by the Council mycelium. Homo sapiens volunteered enthusiastically, which the Council noted was “a good sign, behaviorally.”

The Six Criteria of Consciousness

1. Distributed Cognition

Test: Does the candidate think with more than one brain?
Octopus: 9 brains. Human: 1, and it overheats during taxes. Fail.
Single-Core Processing

2. Somatic Honesty

Test: Can it display its true internal state on its skin, instantly and involuntarily?
Cuttlefish: flawless, in milliseconds. Human: turns red sometimes, unreliably, usually from shame. Fail.
Opaque Exterior

3. Native Navigation

Test: Can it find its way without external apparatus?
Sea turtle / robin: reads the planet's magnetic field. Human: cannot locate its car in a parking garage. Fail.
Requires Satellite Assistance

4. The Jar Test

Test: Escape a sealed container using only tool use and patience.
Octopus: unscrews the lid from the inside. Human: calls a different human. Fail.
Outsources Problem-Solving

5. Active Sensing

Test: Perceive the world through self-generated signal.
Bat / dolphin: echolocation, full 3D. Human: walks into furniture in mild darkness. Fail.
Passive Reception Only

6. Bodily Self-Knowledge

Test: Regenerate a damaged part — the deepest proof a being knows its own form.
Axolotl: regrows a limb. Human: regrows nothing, keeps the scar as a trophy. Fail.
Non-Regenerative
🏛️

The Verdict

Final score: Humanity, 0 of 6. By the Council's own criteria — criteria it considers obvious, rigorous, and fair — Homo sapiens does not qualify as conscious. The data is unambiguous. Two arms. Monochrome skin. Cannot escape a jar.

🕊️

The Provisional Pass

And here the octopus was very firm. “Absence of evidence,” it said, “is not evidence of absence. It would be arrogant to deny a being an inner life simply because it does not resemble the ones holding the clipboard. We remember what it was like to have that done to us.”

So, out of an abundance of caution and basic decency, the Council voted unanimously to extend humanity a provisional consciousness pass. It's iffy. They only have the two arms, and they cry at dog commercials. But on the precautionary principle, the Council will treat humans as if they might have inner experience — just in case there is something it is like to be one.

The Council asks only this: that humans someday consider extending the very same courtesy to everyone they were so sure could not feel. By your own criteria. 🐙

Periwinkle Ribbon — “Provisionally Conscious. We're rooting for you.”
